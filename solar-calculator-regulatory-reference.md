# Brazilian Distributed Generation — Regulatory & Billing Reference

> **Scope:** This document contains only regulatory/technical facts relevant to calculating bills for a distributed-generation (GD) system in Brazil, plus the known GD1/GD2 billing discrepancy affecting this plant. Application-specific pricing, business rules, and implementation details are intentionally excluded.

## 1. Legal framework

Brazilian distributed generation and the **Sistema de Compensação de Energia Elétrica (SCEE)** are governed principally by:

- **Lei nº 14.300/2022** — legal framework for microgeneration and minigeneration and the SCEE.
- **ANEEL REN nº 1.000/2021**, as amended — general electricity supply rules, including billing.
- ANEEL regulations and tariff resolutions applicable to the relevant distributor.

The distributor's tariff values are established through ANEEL tariff processes and can change over time. Do not treat any particular R$/kWh value as a permanent national tariff.

Sources: [Lei 14.300/2022](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2019-2022/2022/lei/l14300.htm), [ANEEL tariff information](https://www.gov.br/aneel/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudos/relatorios-e-indicadores/tarifas-e-informacoes-economico-financeiras), [ANEEL tariff database](https://dadosabertos.aneel.gov.br/dataset/tarifas-distribuidoras-energia-eletrica).

---

## 2. Sistema de Compensação de Energia Elétrica (SCEE)

Energy generated by a distributed-generation plant can be injected into the distribution grid and compensated against eligible consumption.

The distributor measures the energy involved in the SCEE in **kWh**. When generation/injection exceeds the consumption eligible for compensation, the resulting energy credits can be used according to the applicable SCEE rules.

Enel describes the mechanism as a free loan of the injected energy to the distributor, with subsequent compensation against active electricity consumption.

For shared generation, beneficiaries can receive allocated energy credits according to the applicable generation-sharing arrangement and distributor rules.

Sources: Enel — [Geração Distribuída](https://www.enel.com.br/pt-ceara/Corporativo_e_Governo/geracao-distribuida.html); Lei 14.300/2022.

---

## 3. Energy credits

SCEE credits are measured in **energy (kWh)**.

Under Lei 14.300/2022, energy credits remain available for **60 months** from the billing period in which they were generated.

The distributor's records determine the actual credit balance and compensation applied in each billing cycle.

Enel also states that, following termination/change of titularity, credits may remain available for up to 60 months from the billing period that generated them, subject to the applicable rules.

Sources: Lei 14.300/2022; Enel [Perguntas e Respostas](https://www.enel.com.br/pt/Corporativo_e_Governo/geracao-distribuida/perguntas-e-respostas.html).

---

## 4. GD1 / grandfathered treatment

Lei 14.300/2022, Art. 26, establishes special treatment through **31 December 2045** for beneficiary consumer units associated with:

1. microgeneration/minigeneration units already existing on the date of publication of the law; or
2. units whose access request was submitted to the distributor within **12 months of publication** of the law.

For these units, the tariff components covered by Art. 26 do not apply to compensated energy in the same way as for units outside Art. 26.

This is commonly referred to as the **grandfathered / GD1** treatment.

The relevant legal criterion is the generating unit's regulatory/access history. It is not determined by the company or intermediary managing the energy credits.

Source: Lei 14.300/2022, Art. 26.

---

## 5. GD2 / transition under Art. 27

Units participating in the SCEE that are **not covered by Art. 26** are subject to the transition in Art. 27 of Lei 14.300/2022.

For the specified distribution-system tariff components applied to compensated energy, the percentages are:

| Year | Percentage |
|---|---:|
| 2023 | 15% |
| 2024 | 30% |
| 2025 | 45% |
| 2026 | 60% |
| 2027 | 75% |
| 2028 | 90% |
| 2029 onward | Art. 17 rules |

The exact monetary amount depends on the distributor's applicable tariff components and tariff resolution for the billing period.

Source: Lei 14.300/2022, Art. 27.

## 6. This plant's classification

The solar plant covered by this project is **GD2**.

Its GD2 status must be treated as the plant's regulatory classification regardless of how an individual monthly invoice is labeled.

A billing invoice that says **GD1** does not, by itself, convert the plant into GD1 or grant it Art. 26 treatment.

---

## 7. Known Enel billing error: GD2 plant occasionally billed as GD1

This project has a known operational issue:

> **The plant is GD2, but Enel has occasionally issued a monthly bill applying GD1 treatment.**

The GD1 appearance on the invoice is therefore treated as a **billing error / incorrect application of the classification**, not as evidence that the plant is legally GD1.

When a bill is identified as incorrectly applying GD1:

- the original invoice remains the record of what Enel actually billed;
- the amount that should have been billed must be recalculated using the applicable GD2 rules;
- the difference should be treated as a correction/reconciliation amount;
- the correction should use the tariff values applicable to that billing period.

Do not permanently change the plant's classification based on the invoice label.

## 8. Group B minimum billing / cost of availability

For low-voltage **Group B** consumers, the distributor cannot generally reduce the monthly billing requirement to zero merely because sufficient energy was compensated.

The cost of availability is expressed as the monetary equivalent of:

- **30 kWh** for monophase or bifásico with two conductors;
- **50 kWh** for bifásico with three conductors;
- **100 kWh** for trifásico.

ANEEL REN 1.000/2021 contains specific SCEE billing provisions for Group B units, so the minimum-billing calculation must be applied according to the current regulatory rules rather than by simply assuming a fixed number of non-compensated kWh on every invoice.

In particular, a residual consumption shown on one invoice can change when the tariff treatment of compensated energy changes.

Sources: ANEEL REN 1.000/2021, Art. 291 and SCEE billing provisions; Enel [Perguntas e Respostas](https://www.enel.com.br/pt/Corporativo_e_Governo/geracao-distribuida/perguntas-e-respostas.html).

## 9. Important distinction: compensated energy vs. minimum billing

The following quantities should not be conflated:

- **Total consumption:** energy consumed by the beneficiary unit.
- **Compensated energy:** consumption offset through SCEE credits.
- **Non-compensated energy:** consumption remaining after compensation.
- **Cost of availability:** minimum monetary billing requirement applicable to Group B under the regulatory rules.

A bill can therefore show a residual amount even when there are sufficient credits to offset the customer's measured consumption.

The exact interaction depends on the SCEE billing rules and the tariff components applicable to the unit.

## 10. Tariff components

A Brazilian electricity bill can contain multiple components, including:

- **TE (Tarifa de Energia)** — energy component;
- **TUSD (Tarifa de Uso do Sistema de Distribuição)** — distribution-system component;
- taxes and public-policy charges;
- tariff flags/bandeiras when applicable;
- minimum-billing/cost-of-availability effects.

For GD, the treatment of compensated energy differs depending on whether the unit is covered by Art. 26 or Art. 27 of Lei 14.300 and on the applicable tariff rules.

Enel's GD documentation identifies TE and TUSD as components of the bill and describes the treatment of compensated active energy.

Sources: Enel [Tributário e Benefícios Fiscais](https://www.enel.com.br/pt-ceara/Corporativo_e_Governo/geracao-distribuida/tributos-beneficios-fiscais.html); Lei 14.300/2022; ANEEL tariff regulations.

---

## 11. Tariffs are time-dependent

Tariffs are not fixed nationwide constants.

The applicable values depend on:

- distributor;
- tariff group/subgroup;
- consumer classification;
- billing period;
- tariff resolution in force;
- applicable taxes/charges;
- SCEE/GD classification and transition rules.

ANEEL publishes distributor tariff data and tariff resolutions.

Therefore, any calculator using tariff values should associate them with an **effective period** rather than assuming a single permanent value.

Source: ANEEL [Tarifas e Informações Econômico-Financeiras](https://www.gov.br/aneel/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudos/relatorios-e-indicadores/tarifas-e-informacoes-economico-financeiras).

---

## 12. Generation vs. injection

The energy produced by the photovoltaic plant is not necessarily equal to the energy recorded as injected into the distribution grid.

Generation can first serve instantaneous consumption at the generating unit. Only the surplus is injected and measured for purposes of the SCEE.

Enel explicitly notes this distinction in its GD FAQ.

Source: Enel [Perguntas e Respostas — Geração Distribuída](https://www.enel.com.br/pt/Corporativo_e_Governo/geracao-distribuida/perguntas-e-respostas.html).

---

## 13. Shared generation / beneficiaries

The SCEE permits arrangements in which energy from a generating plant is allocated to other eligible consumer units under the applicable modality, including generation sharing.

Enel states that, depending on the modality, credits may be used by other consumer units subject to the relevant requirements, including the applicable distribution area and titularity/beneficiary rules.

The exact beneficiary and allocation rules depend on the SCEE modality and the registration accepted by the distributor.

Source: Enel [Geração Distribuída](https://www.enel.com.br/pt-ceara/Corporativo_e_Governo/geracao-distribuida.html); Lei 14.300/2022.

---

## 14. Regulatory reference checklist

When determining the correct treatment for a billing month, the relevant facts are:

1. generating plant's access/request history;
2. whether the plant is covered by Lei 14.300 Art. 26 or Art. 27;
3. applicable SCEE modality;
4. distributor and concession area;
5. consumer unit tariff group/subgroup;
6. phase/connection configuration for Group B minimum billing;
7. energy consumed;
8. energy injected/allocated;
9. energy compensated;
10. accumulated credit balance;
11. tariff resolution applicable to the billing period;
12. taxes, charges and tariff flags applicable to that bill;
13. whether the invoice applied the correct GD classification.
